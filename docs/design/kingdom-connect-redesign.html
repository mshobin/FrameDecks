A

Executive UX review

Kingdom Connect already does the hard part well. A meeting is a list of scenes, a scene is a stack of layers, and one canvas shows what Zoom and Teams receive. The problems in 6.2 were in the frame around that model, not in the model itself:

  • Two horizontal bars. The header (navigation, search, status) and the bottom live bar (Previous, Go Live, Next, transition, program) together took about 124 px of every screen. The live controls sat at the bottom, far from the status they affect.
  • The preview was the smallest panel. Fixed side columns of 264 px and 340 px left the canvas 762 px wide on a 1366 px laptop, the most common church PC screen. The canvas is the one thing a leader must always see.
  • Nothing could be resized or put away. A leader with 40 scenes and one with 4 got the same proportions.
  • "On air" was said five times. In the header pill, under Go Live, on the canvas badge, on the scene card and in the Inspector. Each is useful on its own, but together they are noise.
  • The phone remote had three layouts named after jobs (Presenter, Leader, Producer). People pick by confidence, not by job title, and even the simplest layout still had seven controls.
  • No team space. Co-leaders coordinate in WhatsApp or Zoom chat, outside the tool, often in view of the participants.

Verdict: keep the Scene → Layer → Canvas model and redesign the frame. That means one command bar, a 2×2 elastic workspace that gives the preview the most room, a contextual Inspector, a phone remote with Simple, Standard and Advanced modes, and a secure team space as the next major feature.

F

Collaboration architecture proposed

Today Kingdom Connect is a program on one PC, and phones on the same Wi-Fi control it. Co-leaders are usually at home on other networks, so a team space needs a small relay service on the internet. The relay passes encrypted messages between devices; it never sees what was written. The meeting PC stays in charge of what is on screen.

Placement

  • Desktop: a 💬 button in the command bar with an unread count. It opens a right-edge drawer (360 px) that docks as a third column on screens ≥1600 px. In Presentation Mode it shows only incoming requests and mentions.
  • Phone: a Team tab in Standard and Advanced. Simple mode shows a single banner for messages from the host, with quick reactions.
  • In context: any message can be attached to a scene ("comment on Discussion 2"); the scene card shows a 💬 badge. Requests to go live appear on the host's GO LIVE button.

What it does

Direct messages; group and meeting-specific rooms (one room per meeting, created with it); presence (online, away, offline, "live controller"); quick reactions; shared notes (one per meeting and per scene, synced with the scene's leader notes); shared action lists (assign, due, done); notifications (desktop toast and phone push, content-free); sync across desktop and phone (each device is a member in its own right).

Technical: the meeting PC enforces roles on its own server, because hiding a button on a phone is not security. The relay enforces only membership. Scalability: the relay is stateless apart from its queues and scales horizontally. MLS keeps group key updates efficient (logarithmic in group size), which matters for rooms of 50+ leaders across a church network.

Threat model in one line: a stranger on church Wi-Fi, a lost phone, a curious relay operator, and a mistaken tap during a live meeting. The first three are handled by pairing, revocation and end-to-end encryption. The fourth by roles, confirmations on Blackout/Emergency in Simple mode, and HIDE ALL always within reach.

H

Panel resizing strategy built

A CSS grid with three named sizes: the left column width, the preview's share of the right column, and the Scenes share of the left column. Dragging changes only those three CSS variables, once per animation frame, so nothing is re-laid out in JavaScript. When a drag ends, the preview is refitted once.

ModeHow you get itWhat happens
DockedDefaultScenes / Layers left (300 px), Preview 64% over Inspector 36%
ExpandedDrag any border; arrows on a focused border (Shift = 3×); double-click or Home to resetLeft column 220–560 px; Preview 30–85%; Scenes 20–85%
FoldedThe fold button in a panel's header, Enter on its border, or the Layout menuThe panel becomes its header strip; its neighbour takes the room; click the strip to unfold
Hidden column‹ next to "Scenes"Left column goes; a handle on the edge brings it back
Auto-hideLayout menu → Auto-hide the InspectorThe Inspector stays a strip and opens over the bottom of the preview while the pointer or keyboard is in it
Full screen (preview only)The ⤢ button or Layout menuThe preview fills the workspace; the command bar stays

Why this approach: CSS grid with variables costs nothing at rest and is smooth on older church PCs (no layout thrash; iframes are disabled while dragging so the pointer is never lost). Choices are stored per PC, because a projector booth and a home laptop differ. Scalability: a fifth panel (Team) is one more grid column and one more splitter, with the same code.

Why it beats the 6.2 layout:

  • The preview is the largest region at every size, and the operator can make it larger in one gesture.
  • Every live control is in one line at eye level, next to the status it changes.
  • The Inspector appears only when there is something to edit, so the Studio looks calm during a meeting and complete during preparation.
  • The same structure carries to tablets (stacked) and phones (the remote), so leaders learn it once.
  • It leaves a clean slot for the team space without another rearrangement.
